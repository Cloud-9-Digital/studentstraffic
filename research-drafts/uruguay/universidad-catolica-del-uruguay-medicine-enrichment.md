# Universidad Católica del Uruguay — Doctor/a en Medicina

**Research status:** held — high-density applicant packet; not yet cleared for publishing.

## Decision summary

Universidad Católica del Uruguay (UCU) offers an in-person **Doctor/a en Medicina** at its Montevideo campus. The programme is six years, has a morning timetable, and accepts completed secondary education from every orientation with no outstanding subjects. UCU identifies the degree as a university-level programme recognised by Uruguay's Ministry of Education and Culture (MEC).

The course is a realistic option for an applicant seeking a Spanish-medium, campus-based medical degree with early case work, a clinical-learning route and a final supervised internship. It is **not** decision-ready on price: UCU's public medicine page directs applicants to request economic information, but does not publish a current total programme fee, annual fee or instalment schedule. Do not show an invented fee or a “starting from” amount.

## Programme and learning model

The programme is structured as a basic cycle, clinical cycle and pre-professional practice. UCU says 92% of courses are compulsory and 8% are elective. It describes problem-based learning, a common Health cycle, case work from the beginning of the degree, and facilities that include simulation, morphofunctional and human-movement laboratories.

The degree requires English at an equivalent B2 level in speaking, writing and reading for graduation. UCU says it provides no-cost courses for students who need to reach that level. The Medicine timetable is basic cycle: morning and afternoon; clinical cycle: morning, with some compulsory afternoon activities. This should be presented as an attendance-planning fact, not as a casual “flexible schedule” claim.

## Admissions and international documents

For undergraduate entry, UCU starts the process with a request for economic information and an academic interview. To complete registration, the applicant signs the contract and pays the first instalment; UCU says that payment reserves the programme place and is non-refundable if the applicant cancels.

Applicants who completed secondary school abroad must submit:

- a school certificate confirming completion of secondary education and eligibility for tertiary/university study;
- a transcript listing subjects and passing grades; and
- documents apostilled for countries party to the Hague Apostille Convention, or legalised where applicable.

The programme page separately states the academic eligibility standard as completed secondary education from any orientation, with no pending subjects. The current public pages do not establish a Medicine-specific application deadline, Spanish-language requirement, visa workflow, admissions test/interview rules, or an exhaustive document list for every nationality. Those must not be implied.

## Fees, discounts and scholarships

### Current fee state: on request

UCU confirms that degree studies are paid through equal, consecutive instalments. The instalments cover the programme's total credits plus a stated allowance of additional credits; extra credits are charged at the current credit price. UCU does not show the current Medicine fee, the number of instalments, any all-in total, or whether clinical/internship costs are included on the public pages reviewed for this packet.

The admissions page advertises time-bound reductions on the **first instalment** for an August 2026 start (50% through 30 June 2026; 30% through 15 July 2026), plus some eligibility-based discounts. These are not the programme fee and should never be presented as one. They must be rechecked before use because their dates and eligibility can change.

UCU's annual scholarship competition includes Medicine among participating undergraduate programmes. It advertises more than 400 new undergraduate scholarships annually. Its published categories include academic-excellence awards of 100% or 80%, and need-based Dámaso Antonio Larrañaga awards of 100% or 80%, subject to the relevant criteria and competition process. The currently published schedule includes later competition dates for a March 2027 start; this is a future intake reference, not a guarantee that a particular applicant will receive funding. Scholarship holders have continuing academic and participation requirements.

## Clinical learning and internship

UCU's programme describes practice through its university clinic, community settings and public/private institutions. The university's April 2025 report on the first internship cohort gives a more specific, source-backed description: the final internship ran for 40 weeks, with four ten-week rotations under professor supervision. Three rotations were in Montevideo and one was compulsory outside Montevideo.

For that reported cohort, the Montevideo training covered Internal Medicine and Paediatrics, Surgery/Gynaecology, and an elective designed around the student's intended professional direction. UCU listed agreements with Círculo Católico, Asociación Española, CASMU, Médica Uruguaya, Hospital Evangélico, Hospital de Tacuarembó (ASSE), Asistencial Médica San José and COMECA. Interns worked in areas including outpatient clinics, emergency shifts and inpatient care, and were assessed by a tutor with fortnightly meetings with specialty professors.

This is strong evidence of the model and named partners at the time of publication. It is not a promise that every future student receives a particular site, specialty or elective; placement arrangements and rotation availability remain cohort-dependent.

## Recognition and professional pathway

UCU states that Doctor/a en Medicina is a university-level course recognised by Uruguay's MEC. That establishes the programme's domestic institutional recognition claim. It does **not** establish eligibility for registration, licensure, residency, examination or practice in another country. Any target-market professional pathway requires country-specific assessment before a public claim can be made.

## Student support and mobility

UCU's Bienestar Estudiantil service is available to UCU students and provides orientation, inclusion support, wellbeing promotion and scholarship-holder follow-up. Its orientation service supports academic adjustment and learning difficulties through individual interviews, group interventions and workshops.

UCU also publishes Medicine mobility destinations and describes regional and international student exchange. Availability, host approval, language, travel cost and clinical-rotation eligibility vary by destination; mobility should be positioned as an opportunity, not a guaranteed component of the degree. No Medicine-specific UCU accommodation offer or published living-cost budget was verified for this packet.

## Applicant FAQs

### How long is Medicine at UCU?

The Doctor/a en Medicina programme is six years, delivered in person at UCU's Montevideo campus.

### Is there a published 2026 Medicine fee?

No exact current Medicine fee was publicly displayed in the reviewed UCU pages. UCU provides economic information through its admissions process and uses instalments for degree payment. A cost plan needs the current written Medicine quotation and confirmation of what the instalments include.

### What does an international secondary-school graduate need?

UCU asks for evidence of completed secondary education that qualifies the applicant for university study, a subject-and-grades transcript, and apostille or legalisation as applicable. The admissions team must confirm the country-specific checklist and whether any translation or further step applies.

### Is the degree recognised in Uruguay?

UCU states that the degree is recognised by Uruguay's MEC as a university-level programme. This is not the same as professional recognition in another country.

### What is the internship like?

UCU reported a 40-week supervised internship for its first cohort, split into four ten-week rotations, with three Montevideo rotations and one compulsory rotation outside Montevideo. Clinical locations and exact rotations can change by cohort.

### Can Medicine students apply for scholarships?

Yes. Medicine participates in UCU's undergraduate scholarship competition. Published awards include 100% and 80% categories, but awards depend on eligibility, results, available places and ongoing conditions.
