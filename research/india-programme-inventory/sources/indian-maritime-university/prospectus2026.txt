      India stands today at a defining moment in its maritime journey as itis moving
      steadily towards becoming a strong and self-reliant maritime nation. Under the
      guidance of the Maritime Amrit Kaal Vision (MAKV) 2047 and the roadmap laid
      down in the Maritime India Vision (MIV) 2030, the maritime sector has been
      identified as a key pillar of national growth, global trade, and sustainable
      development.

      Under MAKV, the maritime sector has been identified as a powerful engine of
      economic growth, strategic strength, sustainability, and global leadership. With over
      11098 km of coastline, a vast network of ports and inland waterways, and nearly 95%
      of India’s trade by volume and 65% by value carried through sea routes, the maritime
      domain is central to India’s future.

      The Maritime India Vision (MIV) 2030 and India’s Maritime Vision lay out a
      comprehensive roadmap for transforming the sector through modern port
      infrastructure, green and smart shipping, shipbuilding and ship recycling, coastal
      and inland water transport, maritime logistics, digitalisation, safety, and skilled
      manpower development. These national visions emphasize not only infrastructure
      and technology, but also the critical role of human capital in shaping a resilient and
      globally competitive maritime ecosystem.

      The maritime sector today offers exciting and meaningful careers, both at sea and
      on shore. Opportunities exist in ship operations, ports, logistics, shipbuilding,
      maritime safety, environmental protection, research, and policy support. These
      careers provide global exposure, professional growth, and a chance to directly
      contribute to nation building.

      At the heart of this transformation stands the Indian Maritime University (IMU) —
      India’s premier institution dedicated exclusively to maritime education, training,
      research, and innovation. IMU plays a pivotal role in developing the skilled
      professionals required to realise national maritime goals. Through its undergraduate,
      postgraduate, doctoral and diploma programmes, IMU nurtures talent across core
      maritime disciplines such as nautical science, marine engineering, naval
      architecture, port and shipping management, logistics, maritime law, ocean
      engineering, maritime safety, and coastal studies.

      IMU’s academic framework is closely aligned with national priorities, industry
      requirements, and global standards. Our programmes integrate theoretical
      knowledge with hands-on training, advanced simulation, sea-time exposure, industry

1. INTRODUCTION
The Indian Maritime University (IMU), headquartered in Chennai, was established on 14th
November 2008 as a teaching-cum-affiliating university under the aegis of the Ministry of
Ports, Shipping, and Waterways Government of India. It was set up to promote maritime
studies, training, research and extension with focus on emerging areas like oceanography,
maritime history, maritime laws, maritime security, search and rescue, transportation of
dangerous cargo, environmental studies and other related fields, and to achieve excellence
in these areas. The oldest of the IMU Campuses i.e. IMU Navi Mumbai Campus was
established in 1927 in it’s earliest avatar of TS Dufferin.
IMU has 6 Campuses at Chennai, Kochi, Kolkata, Mumbai Port, Navi Mumbai and
Visakhapatnam.

 IMU conducts the Common Entrance Test (IMU-CET) every year on All India basis for
 admissions to its Programmes for IMU campuses, Affiliated Institutes and Non-Affiliated
 Institutes with IMU.

 As per DGS Training Circular 12 of 2020, dated 01.04.2020, all Maritime Training Institutes
 [MTIs] regulated by DG Shipping are required to admit candidates on the basis of IMU-CET
 ranking and as amended from time to time. IMU-CET is mandatory for admission to DGS
 approved Programmes.

 Hence Candidates seeking admission in Pre-Sea Courses Diploma in Nautical Science,
 B.Sc. (Nautical -Science) and B.E / B.Tech. (Marine Engineering) to any MTI (Govt. or
 Private- Affiliated to any University) are required to qualify in IMU-CET.

  https://www.dgshipping.gov.in/Content/MaritimeHealthBranch.aspx
  For the above 4 Marine Programmes, the Eye Sight requirements are specified by
  the Directorate General of Shipping. All the candidates must meet these
  requirements and will have to produce certificate of eye sight and hearing from a

  medical standards before applying and filling the preferences of courses as all DG
  approved courses require meeting fully the specified medical standards. In case of
  any candidate getting rejected on account of failing in medical standards, his fee
  will not be refunded. Hence it is advised that the candidate should get his/her
  medical standards tested first before applying for counselling where he/she needs
  to fill the course choices. Candidates not meeting medical standards should not
  elect these four Marine courses as they would eventually be rejected at a later
  stage. However his/her request for change of course may be accepted subject to
  availability of seats.
  List of approved doctors for issuing the certificate is available at :
  https://www.dgshipping.gov.in/Content/MaritimeHealthBranch.aspx
B) For Programmes: Other than above four programmes:
   A physical fitness certificate issued by a Registered Medical Practitioner is
   required to be submitted at the time of certificate verification for admission,
   when selected.
Note: Candidates must satisfy themselves about their eligibility for admission. No Fee
(non-refundable category) will be refunded on account of medical ineligibility.

11. IMU CET AND ADMISSION MECHANISM IN IMU
CAMPUSES

   IMU conducts a Common Entrance Test (IMU-CET) every year on All India basis for
   admissions to its Programme for IMU campuses and Affiliated Institutes. As per
   DGS Training Circular 12 of 2020, dated 01.04.2020, all Maritime Training Institutes

18. ANTI RAGGING
   IMU has a zero tolerance policy towards ragging. It follows UGC Regulations and Hon’ble
   Supreme Court guidelines.

   The ragging has inter-alia been defined as any conduct by any student/students whether by
   words spoken or written or an act which has an effect of teasing, treating or handling with
   rudeness a fresher or any other student or indulging in rowdy or undisciplined activities which
   causes or is likely to cause annoyance, hardship or psychological harm or creates fear or
   apprehension thereof in any fresher or any other student or forcing a student to do an act
   which has the effect of causing or generating a sense of shame or torment or embarrassment
   so as to affect the physique or psyche of a fresher or any other student, showing of power,
   authority or superiority by a student to any fresher or any other student.

   Collective gathering of students with any form of excuse and interviewing the fresher’s will
   also be treated as an act of ragging.

   Campus /Affiliated Institutes authorities may be contacted to report any ragging activities.

   UGC helpline No.1800-180-5522 may also be contacted to report ragging incidents.

21.ADMISSION OF FOREIGN STUDENTS
 Foreign students can get admission to certain programmes through two modes :
   a) IMU Direct mode - This mode is being introduced from AY 2026-27 onwards.
   b) DASA mode - existing mode
 The details of each mode are elaborated in succeeding paragraphs.
 22.A. DIRECT ADMISSION
 IMU will admit foreign national students in Non-DGS UG programmes and PG programmes
 as part of internationalisation of higher education to foster the development of global
 citizens through student and scholar mobility.

 Residential Requirement:
 a) For UG Programmes:
 Students who satisfy either of the following conditions are eligible for
 undergraduate (UG) Programmes:
 (i) Any foreign national studying in India or abroad.
 (ii) An Indian Citizen who completed their 11th and 12th grades from an institution abroad.


 b) For PG Programmes:
 Students who satisfy at least one of the following conditions are eligible
 for postgraduate (PG) Programmes:
 (i) Any foreign national studying in India or abroad.
 (ii) An Indian Citizen who completed their undergraduate studies abroad.
 (iii) An Indian Citizen who completed their undergraduate
 programme in India but completed their 11 th and 12 th grades abroad.


 c) How to apply?
 Separate portal will be available on IMU Website.

 Age:
    Date of birth as recorded in the Passport/ Birth Certificate or any certificate issued by
    the Government authorities only will be taken as authentic.
     For UG programmes Maximum age: (As on 01.08.2026)
    Male : 25 years
    Female/Transgender : 27 years
    For PG Programmes there is no age restriction

Seat allotment procedure:
    The allotment of a programme will be made as per the choices indicated by the applicant
    in his/herapplication form.
    An applicant appearing higher on merit list will have a higher priority in the allotment of
    their programme choices compared to an applicant appearing lower in the merit list.
    If the first preference indicated by the applicant is not available, they will be allotted the
    secondpreference and so on.
    Applicants who are not allotted any seat will be marked as “Not allotted” in the allotment
    list and may be considered for subsequent rounds of allotment if vacancies arise.
    All Admitted foreign candidates are required to report on 15th July 2026 tentatively.

VISA and travel requirements:
    All selected Foreign nationals must obtain a valid student visa, applicable to their
    category, from the Indian Missions abroad. The visa application should be submitted
    along with the provisional admission offer letter and other required documents at the
    respective Indian Mission in their home country.
    It is the responsibility of the candidate to arrange for the appropriate visa.
    Upon receiving admission, candidates must submit their student visa within one month of
    completing the admission formalities.
    Failure to obtain the visa within the stipulated time will result in the cancellation of
    admission.
    Upon arrival in India, every foreign student must register with the Foreigners Regional
    Registration Officer (FRRO).
