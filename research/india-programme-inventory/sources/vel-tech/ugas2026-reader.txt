> 26.6

Notional Credit Hours (As per the National Credit Framework (NCrF)) 

a) ‘Notional Credit-hours’ is defined as the total contact hours and self-learning hours put together. Notional Credit-hours is 30 per credit for all courses uniformly. b) If the number of credits offered in a semester is 20 Credits, then the total notional credit hours utilized for teaching and learning must be around 600 (20 x 30) hours, which includes physical contact hours and self-learning hours.  

> 26.6.1

Course Credit Hours 

The academic programmes of the Institution (Deemed to be University) are based on credit system. A ‘credit’ is the unit by which the course work is mea-sured. It determines the number of contact hours of instructions required per week. The general credit allocation pattern is as follows: • 1 Hour of Lecture (L) / Tutorial (T) per week is considered as 1 credit (15 Hours/Semester) • 2 Hours of Practical (P)/Project (PJ) per week is assigned with 1 credit (30 Hours/ Semester)  

> 26.6.2

Self-Learning(SL) Hours 

In addition to lecture, tutorial, the curriculum includes Self Learning Hours. Self-Learning Hours refer to the time students are expected to dedicate to inde-pendent learning activities outside of scheduled classroom instruction. These activities may include reviewing lecture notes, completing assignments, engag-ing in further reading, practicing problems, or working on projects without di-rect guidance of faculty. The self learning methods are • Experiential Learning. • National/International Collaborative Learnings.

> 26.9

Curriculum Components  

> 26.9.1

Programme Core Courses (PCC) 

"Programme Core Courses (PCC)" for students refer to a set of courses that are essential for their respective discipline. It carries 66 credits for 3 year degree and 68 credits for 4 year honours degree. It is designed to impart discipline-specific knowledge, technical competencies, and employability skills essential for professional growth and career readiness in the chosen field of study.  

> 26.9.2

Programme Elective Courses (PEC) 

“Program Elective Courses (PEC)" for students typically refer to specialized courses offered within their respective program that allow students to choose subjects related to their specific major or specialization. It carries 18 credits for 3 year degree and 38 credits for 4 year honours degree. PEC courses provide students with the flexibility to tailor their specific domains according to their career goals or passions while still covering their core principles.  

> 26.9.3

Multidisciplinary / Open Elective Courses (OEC) 

"Open Elective Courses (OEC)" are to be taken from courses offered by other school only, outside the student’s major discipline but not from own depart-ment. Students shall study 6 credits for three year degree and 9 credits for honours degree under Open Elective Category. These courses enhance the aca-demic experience, foster Multidisciplinary and interdisciplinary learning, and

contribute to the personal and professional development of students, prepar-ing them to meet diverse challenges in their careers.  

> 26.9.4

Ability Enhancement Courses (AEC) 

a) The AEC courses are the courses based upon the content that leads to Knowl-edge enhancement. These courses are a set of value based courses that aim to bring constructive behavioral changes to students. Each course carries 1 or 2 credits. Totally, 8 credits for three year degree and 12 credits for 4-year honors degree are to be earned under this category. b) The department is responsible for scheduling, conducting the courses along with 100% of Evaluation. The evaluation mark shall be submitted to the of-fice of the Dean Academics. c) Apart from the above, the student shall complete an extension activity as community service (volunteer work in NGO) or take part in the extension activities of the department during any one of the semesters between I and V. The student shall earn one (1) credit on completion of extension activ-ity which may be verified with his certificate of completion from the con-cerned department/ organization.  

> 26.9.5

Skill Enhancement Courses (SEC) 

1) Skill Enhancement Courses (SEC) are intended to equip the students with discipline specific practical skills that complement their core area of study. These courses focus on enhancing applied knowledge and professional com-petencies to improve employability and readiness for real-world challenges. Students shall study 18 credits for three year degree and 30 credits for hon-ours degree under this Category. The following courses are required to be offered for all programmes of Arts, Science, Commerce and Management. 2) Departments shall be empowered to design and offer program-specific SEC courses, including emerging and interdisciplinary domains such as Artifi-cial Intelligence, Blockchain, and related areas, subject to approval by the competent academic bodies. 

a) Summer Internship/Internship 

Students shall undergo an internship in reputed industries or organiza-tions for a minimum period of four (4) weeks during any summer vacation after the completion of the First Year. The internship shall carry four (4) credits and is mandatory for the successful completion of the programme.

b) Major Project-I 

Students shall undergo Project Work during sixth semester which carries 6 credits, for a minimum period of 8 weeks in an organization/industry in the major area pertaining to their programme and approved by the Project Review Committee (PRC). 

c) Major Project-II (Applicable for 4-year Honours Degree Students) 

In case of students opted to obtain 4 year Honours Degree, he/she shall un-dergo one more Project Work during eigth semester which carries 6 credits, for a minimum period of 6 weeks with an organization/industry in the ma-jor area pertaining to their programme and approved by the Project Review Committee (PRC). 

d) Project and/or Courses taken with Institutions Abroad 

Students may be allowed to obtain credits under ILC category for the courses specified in the above clauses (a), (b) and (c) from any Institutions Abroad with prior approval of competent authority, as recommended by the de-partment concerned and the Office of the Dean Academics.  

> 26.9.6

Value Added Courses (VAC) 

Value Added Courses (VAC) are supplementary courses aimed at enhancing students’ practical skills, domain-specific knowledge, and employability beyond the scope of their core curriculum. These courses are designed to provide addi-tional competencies relevant to industry needs and carry 8 credits.  

> 26.9.7

Mandatory Courses (Non-CGPA Courses) 

Mandatory Courses are introduced to support students from varied academic backgrounds in adapting to the academic and professional expectations of So-ciety. These courses may be offered with or without credits; however, the credits earned shall not be considered for computing the CGPA but will be reflected in the grade sheet. The student shall select any three courses from the below men-tioned list under this category. The course shall carry 8 credits each for 3-year degree & 4-year degree programmes. 1. Project Management and Finance 2. Innovation & Entrepreneurship 3. Community Service Project (Mandatory for B.B.A. programme) Any other course recommended by the department may also be considered, subject to the approval of the Board of Studies (BoS).

> 26.10

MOOC based self-learning courses with credit transfer  

> 26.10.1

Approved MOOC Platforms and Process 

i) Permitted MOOC Platforms that are currently approved by UGC/AICTE: (a) SWAYAM (b) NPTEL (c) NPTEL+ (d) SWAYAM Plus (e) NASSCOM Skills Prime (Through MoU with Institution) (f) Other Courses collaboratively offered with Industries (Through MoU) as approved by University Academic Body ii) The list of approved MOOC courses for each semester shall be finalized by the respective departments/schools with the approval of the Board of Stud-ies (BoS) and Academic Council, well in-advance, prior to offer the course. iii) Students must successfully complete the prescribed MOOC courses and submit the pass certificate to be eligible for credit transfer. The marks ob-tained will be converted to the corresponding letter grade and grade point as per the Institution’s guidelines. iv) Upon securing pass marks in the course, the mark obtained will be con-verted as per the criteria specified by Institution (Deemed to be University) and corresponding letter grade and grade point will be assigned by the re-spective department and submitted for further processing. v) The approval process for student-registered MOOC courses begins at the department level and is subsequently submitted to the Office of the Con-troller of Examinations through the office of Dean Academics. vi) Students may take prescribed MOOC courses under Programme Elective Category (PEC)/Open Elective Category (OEC)/SEC/AEC/Non-credit manda-tory courses. vii) Students may register and complete MOOC courses included in the cur-riculum of their programme pertaining to Honours Degree. viii) Students are allowed to register minimum 3 credits to maximum 12 credits. ix) Students who are working with Industries in-person for their Internship, Project, Semester-long Internship, Major Project and On-the-Job Training (OJT) are also allowed to opt for MOOC with credit transfer on the advice of respective department.

> 26.11

Course Registration  

> 26.11.1

Minimum/ Maximum Credit Limits for Regular Course Registration in a semester 

a) The minimum number of credits that the students are expected to register in a semester is 20 credits, and up to a maximum of 25 Credits. b) In case if a student availing semester long internship in any of the semesters, the minimum and maximum credit limit may be relaxed based on the re-quest of the Head of the Department / School Dean and recommendation by any other functional Deans and forwarded by the Dean Academics.  

> 26.11.2
