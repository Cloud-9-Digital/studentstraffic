                                             Preface

The Academy of Maritime Education and Training was established as a private maritime academy
by Dr.J.Ramachandran with an enrolment of 14 cadets on 15th February 1993. Originally it has
offered Higher National Diploma Programme in Marine Engineering and Nautical Science, a
twinning Diploma Course in collaboration with Glasgow College of Nautical Studies (GCNS),
Scotland, U.K. It also offered Diploma in Marine Studies (DMS), Diploma in Nautical Science
(DNS) and Diploma in Shipping & Maritime Transportation (DSMT) in association with Singapore
Maritime Academy. Thereafter AMET introduced specialized degree programs in maritime field
such as 3- year B.Sc., Nautical Science in 2002 and 4 year B.E. Marine Engineering in 2001. On
21st August 2007, Gazette Notification issued by Ministry of Human Resources and Development,
Government of India conferring University status to AMET which is India’s First University in
Maritime Education under section 3 of UGC Act 1956 De Novo category. Since then, the University
is catering the needs and dissemination of knowledge in the field of marine and maritime studies.
Being recognized by Directorate General of Shipping (DGS) for maritime programmes and All India
Council for Technical Education (AICTE) for Engineering programmes and University Grants
Commission (UGC) for all other general programmes for offering various UG, PG, research and
professional training courses, AMET deemed to be University with its truly international standards
and unparalleled quality in education received many national and international recognitions and
awards. AMET deemed to be University dedicated itself to become a World Class University by all
virtue. University strictly following the norms and guidelines of regulatory bodies i.e., UGC, AICTE
and DGS. The Management, administrative and executive authorities and committees like
Executive Council, Academic Council etc. are formed and practiced as per the requirements. The
University is having interest in gaining, shaping and disseminating knowledge in marine and
maritime sector. Students and scholars are encouraged to exercise critical thinking and make
exhaustive inquiry in the specialized knowledge i.e., marine and maritime fields. The University is
having openness and tolerance with no skepticism towards alternate thoughts. The University
reflects the challenges, changes and opportunities in the marine and maritime industry and

QUALITY POLICY
Preamble
Academy of Maritime Education and Training (AMET) has been established as a private maritime
institute with just 14 cadets in 1993. AMET has, through its uncompromising strides of excellence
and ambitious zeal to serve the students and nation, transformed into the first University for
Maritime Education in India under AMET Trust. AMET was granted the deemed to be University
status by the UGC on 21st August 2007 under the “de-novo” category with maritime education as
emerging field with great potential.
In order to provide quality education and training, AMET is continuously upgrading its quality
systems. AMET's quality system was first certified to ISO 9001:1994 in the year 1998. AMET has
upgraded its quality management system and was certified to ISO 9001:2000 version in the year
2001 and was re-certified in the years 2004 and 2007. AMET's Quality Management System was
revised in line with its upgraded status as University and got a revised certificate in December
2008. During 2016, AMET has been re-certified with latest ISO 9001: 2015 standards.
The scope of Quality Management System of AMET deemed to be University for ISO 9001: 2015
certification is as follows:
“Offering maritime and maritime related courses leading to awarding of certificate, diploma,
and degree to students and research scholars.”

                                               Admission Policy


1. Title of the Policy                            : Admission Policy
2. Policy Reference Number                        : AMET/AP/2025/01
3. Effective Date / Review Cycle                  : Academic Year 2025–2026 onwards
Review Cycle                                      : Every 3 years or as mandated by statutory bodies
4. Introduction
AMET Deemed to be University is committed to ensuring a fair, transparent, and merit-based admission
process in accordance with the regulations and guidelines stipulated by statutory bodies such as the
University Grants Commission (UGC), Directorate General of Shipping (DGS), Government of India, and All
India Council for Technical Education (AICTE). The University establishes an Admission Committee
comprising eminent academicians and chaired by a retired High Court Judge to oversee and guide all
admission-related processes, ensuring integrity and accountability.
5. Scope and Applicability
This policy applies to:
       All prospective students seeking admission to various programmes offered by the University
       All undergraduate, postgraduate, diploma, and research programmes
       Admission processes including entrance examinations, interviews, and selection procedures
       All departments and administrative units involved in admission processes
6. Vision, Mission & Quality Policy of the University
Aligned with the vision of AMET University to excel in maritime and technical education, this policy supports
the mission to provide equal access to quality education through a transparent and inclusive admission
system while maintaining academic standards and regulatory compliance.
7. Objectives of the Policy
       Ensure transparency and fairness in the admission process
       Provide equal opportunities to all eligible candidates


                                                                                                                6
      Disseminate accurate and comprehensive admission-related information
      Comply with statutory requirements of UGC, AICTE, and DGS
      Maintain merit-based selection while adhering to reservation norms
      Enhance institutional credibility and stakeholder trust
8. Guiding Principles
      Transparency – Clear and open admission procedures and communication
      Merit-based Selection – Admission based on academic performance and entrance tests
      Inclusiveness – Adherence to Government of India reservation policies
      Accountability – Oversight by Admission Committee and university authorities
      Accessibility – Easy access to application forms and information through online platforms
9. Policy Framework (Step-by-step process)
      Public Notification – Admission details including programmes, eligibility, entrance test, and
       schedules are widely published in national newspapers and on the official university website
      Prospectus Publication – Updated prospectus containing details of courses, eligibility,
       infrastructure, approvals, placement, and academic governance is made available
      Application Submission – Candidates apply online by downloading and submitting completed
       application forms
      Entrance Examination – Candidates appear for the Entrance Test
      Selection Process – Based on academic merit, entrance test performance, and personal interview
      Medical Examination – Mandatory medical fitness tests for programmes approved by DGS
      Publication of Results – Selected candidates list displayed on notice board and communicated
       individually
      Provisional Admission – Registrar issues provisional admission letters to selected candidates
      Waitlist Management – Waitlisted candidates are admitted in case of vacancies
10. Roles and Responsibilities
      Admission Committee – Plans, supervises, and ensures transparency in admission procedures
      Head Admissions– Oversees admission operations and coordination
      Assistant Registrar – Handles documentation and administrative processes
      Registrar – Issues admission letters and maintains official records
      Vice-Chancellor – Final approving authority for admission policies and decisions

4. Introduction
AMET Deemed to be University is committed to establishing a fair, transparent, and regulatory-compliant
fee structure for all its academic programmes, including degree and short-term courses. The fee fixation
process is aligned with the guidelines of the University Grants Commission (UGC), Directorate General of
Shipping (DGS), All India Council for Technical Education (AICTE), and relevant State Government norms.
The University ensures that fee-related practices adhere to UGC (Deemed to be Universities) Regulations,
2016, including provisions related to fee structure, payment, and refund.
5. Scope and Applicability
This policy applies to:
       All students enrolled in undergraduate, postgraduate, diploma, and short-term programmes
       All academic departments and administrative units involved in fee collection and management
       Fee structure determination, revision, payment, and refund processes
All categories of fees including tuition, examination, and special fees
6. Vision, Mission & Quality Policy of the University
In alignment with AMET University’s vision of providing quality maritime and technical education, this policy
ensures affordability, transparency, and accountability in fee practices while maintaining financial
sustainability and compliance with statutory norms.


7. Objectives of the Policy
       Establish a transparent and standardized fee structure
       Ensure compliance with UGC, AICTE, DGS, and Government norms

      Provide clear information on fees and related charges to stakeholders

      Facilitate fair and timely refund mechanisms
      Ensure accountability in financial transactions

      Transparency – All fees are publicly disclosed in prospectus and website
      Regulatory Compliance – Adherence to UGC (Deemed Universities) Regulations, 2016
      Fairness – Fee structure determined based on institutional policies and norms
      Accountability – Proper documentation and issuance of receipts for all payments
      Periodic Review – Regular revision of fee structure by the competent committee
9. Policy Framework
      Constitution of Committee – Fee Fixation Committee constituted as per Government of Tamil Nadu

                                              CONSULTANCY POLICY
1. Title of the Policy                       : Consultancy Policy
2. Policy Reference Number                   : AMET/CP/2025/01
3. Effective Date / Review Cycle             : Academic Year 2025–2026 onwards
Review Cycle                                 : Every 3 years or as mandated by statutory bodies
4. Introduction
The Institution considers consultancy activities as an opportunity to develop mutually beneficial
relationships with industries, academic institutions, government bodies, and other public or private
organizations. These activities aim to provide practical solutions to societal and industrial problems.
Consultancy forms an integral part of the “Knowledge Exchange” portfolio, which is vital for the effective
functioning of higher education institutions. It enhances the professional recognition of faculty members and
contributes to institutional reputation, while also generating revenue. Consultancy services may be offered
under well-defined terms and conditions agreed upon between the client and the consultant. These
services are open to faculty members, research scholars, and students. This policy provides a clear
framework for those engaged in or intending to engage in consultancy activities.
This policy applies to:
       All teaching staff and subject experts of the University
       Consultancy projects undertaken for industry, government, NGOs, and other organizations
       Technical, research-based, advisory, and training consultancy services
       Individual and institutional consultancy assignments
Consultancy Framework
       The University encourages faculty members to undertake consultancy services that facilitate
        knowledge and technology transfer, thereby contributing to economic growth and societal
        development.
       Consultancy services are recognized as a means for professional development of faculty and
        fulfillment of the institution’s social responsibilities.


                                                                                                                 48
       Consultancy involves the application of faculty expertise in areas such as:
             o Providing innovative ideas and solutions
             o Situational analysis and problem-solving
             o Measurement techniques and methodological advancements
             o Development of novel testing and analytical procedures
       Consultancy services must involve clearly defined deliverables, for which the client provides
        financial remuneration.
       Consultancy work is expected to generate financial benefits for both the institution and the
        individuals involved.
       All faculty members are encouraged to undertake at least one consultancy assignment per
        academic year.
       Faculty members must refrain from undertaking any consultancy activity that may directly or
        indirectly harm the reputation of the University.
       Faculty members may avail on-duty leave provisions for consultancy work, subject to institutional
        approval.
       Consultancy activities must not create any conflict of interest with the faculty member’s regular
        academic and administrative responsibilities.
       Details of faculty expertise and consultancy services shall be published on the University website
        to promote visibility and outreach.

Exclusion Clause
The following activities shall not be considered as consultancy services:
       Acting as an external examiner
       Evaluation of answer scripts
       Delivering invited lectures or guest lectures
       Participation in conferences, seminars, or workshops
       Any similar academic engagements

Remuneration received for the above activities shall not fall under consultancy income.
Review of Policy
The University reserves the right to review and revise this policy periodically based on emerging needs,
regulatory requirements, and institutional priorities.

6.1 Semester Based Education


The University has decided to adopt semester based system. The advantages of a semester-based education
include continuous learning and feedback, which improves regular study habits and provides a better understanding
of subjects due to divided workloads and more frequent assessment opportunities. This system also offers flexibility
for designing varied courses, allows for more focused class interaction with increased teacher-student engagement,
and helps prevent excessive academic burden by distributing the syllabus into smaller, more manageable parts.


6.2 Choice Based Credit System


The students are given variety of choices to earn credit to pass the subject. The student can excel in term-end
examination to earn pass grade or they can participate in subject specific events/activities to earn credits. The entire
syllabus is divided into Core, Profession Elective, Open Elective Course, Mandatory and Foundation.he students are

subjects from all the streams. They can select subjects from any discipline. They can also publish a research paper

   3. Implement smart meters and energy audits.

   1. Rainwater harvesting and groundwater recharge.
   2. Water-efficient fixtures in hostels, classrooms, and offices.

   4. Regular water quality monitoring.

   1. Segregation of waste at source into biodegradable, recyclable, and hazardous categories.
   2. Composting of biodegradable waste through vermicomposting/biogas plants.
   3. Recycling of paper, plastic, and e-waste through authorized vendors.
   4. Safe disposal of hazardous waste (chemical, biomedical, oil waste from marine labs/workshops).

   1. Ban on single-use plastics within campus.
   2. Encourage reusable utensils, bottles, and bags.
   3. Promote e-governance and digital documentation to reduce paper use.

AMET Deemed to be University recognizes mentoring as a vital component of student development and
institutional quality enhancement. The University adopts a structured mentorship system to provide
academic, personal, and career guidance to students. As part of this initiative, each student is provided with
a Pragati Edge Book, which serves as a comprehensive mentoring record for tracking academic progress,
personal development, and career planning. The policy aligns with UGC guidelines and NAAC quality
benchmarks to strengthen student support systems.

       Use of mentoring tools such as the Pragati Edge Book

                            INSTITUTIONAL DEVELOPMENT PLAN (IDP) 2025–2030

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

AMET University’s Institutional Development Plan (IDP) 2025–2030 presents a comprehensive roadmap for
institutional growth, guided by the mandate of the University Grants Commission (UGC) and aligned with
the National Education Policy (NEP) 2020. As the University transitions from a specialised maritime
institution to a multidisciplinary university of national and international prominence, the IDP outlines
strategic objectives, implementation pathways, and measurable outcomes that will shape AMET’s
developmental trajectory.

Over the last decade, AMET has consolidated its identity as India’s premier maritime university while
expanding academic presence into engineering, computing, allied health sciences, commerce,
management, and digital education. Significant achievements include hosting the IAMU Annual General
Assembly 2025 (a first in India), receiving the Government of India’s Sagar Ratna Award 2025 for
excellence in maritime education and skill development, and establishing deep industry partnerships with
AP Moller Maersk, V Ships, Fleet Management, Kalvi, FACE Prep, NIAT, Emversity, Global Aviation Pvt
Ltd, Microsoft, IBM, RedHat, and CII.

The IDP recognises this strong foundation and outlines structured development across nine UGC-
mandated components. These include governance reforms, financial sustainability, curriculum
strengthening, teaching–learning innovation, faculty development, enhanced research culture, stronger
industry and international collaborations, infrastructure modernisation, and digital transformation.

The plan is supported by a robust monitoring mechanism through institutional dashboards, IQAC oversight,
Academic and Administrative Audits (AAA), and periodic performance reviews. With clear SMART goals,
performance indicators, and risk mitigation strategies, the IDP provides a credible, outcome-oriented
approach to institutional advancement.

SWOC ANALYSIS

Strengths

AMET University’s greatest strength lies in its unique identity as a comprehensive maritime university
offering a 360-degree coverage of maritime ecosystem disciplines ranging from ship design, construction,
operations, navigation, mechanical and electrical systems, logistics, port operations, ocean science, and
marine biotechnology, to maritime business and allied engineering domains. This integrated ecosystem is
rare among Indian universities. The University’s strong industry partnerships ensure exceptional alignment
between classroom teaching and real-world industry needs. Strategic collaborations with shipping majors
and skill development companies have resulted in consistently high placement rates (including 100% in
core maritime programmes). The University’s robust research culture, supported by significant funded
projects, patents, and a thriving innovation ecosystem (ACIIE), further strengthens its academic standing.
AMET’s international visibility, highlighted by hosting IAMU AGM 2025, distinguishes it within the global
maritime academic community.

Weaknesses

The University’s rapid growth has created a need for proportionate expansion of land and physical
infrastructure. Certain departments require additional senior faculty with global exposure. The research
output, though significant, is concentrated in selected departments and must be broadened. Some
administrative processes require further digitisation and integration under a unified ERP environment.

Opportunities

There is significant scope to expand online and distance education for the global maritime and logistics
workforce. NEP 2020 provides an enabling environment for multidisciplinary expansion, academic flexibility,
internationalisation, and blended learning. Opportunities exist to collaborate with foreign universities for
joint programmes and to establish international offshore centres. The Blue Economy focus of the
Government of India offers new funding possibilities for research and training.

Challenges

Growing competition from national and global players in online and maritime education may impact
enrolment. Rapid technological changes in maritime operations demand frequent curriculum updates.
Regulatory compliance across UGC, DEB, DGS, AICTE, and international bodies must be handled with
precision. Coastal vulnerability to climate risks remains a long-term environmental challenge.
